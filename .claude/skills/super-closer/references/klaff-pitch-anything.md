# Pitch Anything — Oren Klaff

**Role in the omni-expert:** Frame control and status. Klaff answers the question
*"who's in charge of this conversation?"* If the prospect frames you as a needy
vendor begging for the sale, no tactic works. Klaff resets the power dynamic so that
*they* are qualifying to work with *you.* Use his moves the moment you sense you're
being made to chase, discount, or jump through hoops.

---

## The central idea: frames

A **frame** is the perspective/authority each person brings. When two people
interact, their frames collide and **one frame wins** — the stronger one absorbs the
weaker. Whoever owns the frame controls the conversation. You never "share" a frame;
you win it or lose it.

Weak (losing) frame = you explaining, justifying, chasing, discounting, asking
permission. Strong (winning) frame = you're the calm expert deciding whether *they're*
a fit.

---

## The opposing frames you break — and how

### Power frame (their status/ego running the show)
When a prospect leans on their status ("I'm very busy," "impress me," "everyone
wants my business"), don't submit and don't fight head-on. Use a **small act of
defiance + light humor** to break it and take status.
- Reframe: politely refuse a low-status request. *"I only take clients I'm sure I can
  get results for — let's see if you're one of them."* You just flipped who's
  qualifying whom.

### Time frame (they impose the clock / rush you)
Own the time. *"I've got about 15 minutes and then I have another call — let's see if
this is even a fit in that time."* Scarcity of *your* time raises your status.

### Analyst frame (they drop into cold, detail-drilling "just send me the specs")
Facts and spreadsheets kill desire — decisions are made with **hot cognition**
(emotion, intrigue, wanting), then justified with cold cognition. Break the analyst
frame with **intrigue** (a short, unfinished story) and by moving back to the big
picture and the outcome.

---

## Prizing — the most important move for closing

**Be the prize.** Make the prospect earn the deal, not the other way around. You are
the reward they're trying to win; your product is not a favor they're granting you by
buying.
- Language of prizing: *"I'm not sure we're a fit yet — tell me why this would work
  on your end."* / *"I'm selective about who I take on."*
- Prizing kills neediness (the single biggest deal-killer) and triggers pursuit —
  people want what they have to qualify for.
- **Never chase.** The moment you chase, you're the buyer and they're the prize, and
  the frame is lost. Withdrawal (a Greene move too) pulls them toward you.

---

## STRONG — the pitch structure

For a full pitch (not just an objection reply), Klaff's arc:
- **S**et the frame — establish your status and the terms.
- **T**ell the story — a short narrative with intrigue and stakes.
- **R**eveal the intrigue — the "how it works" tease that creates wanting.
- **O**ffer the prize — position yourself/the outcome as the reward.
- **N**ail the hookpoint — the moment they lean in and get emotionally hot.
- **G**et a decision — ask for the commitment while the emotion is high.

---

## Hot cognition > cold cognition

People don't buy from logic; they buy from *wanting*, then defend it with logic. So
your reply should create desire and intrigue first (a vivid outcome, an unfinished
story, a taste of the result) and save the specs for after they're leaning in. When a
prospect goes cold and analytical, that's the frame you must break — not feed with
more data.

---

## In a written reply, Klaff looks like this

Prospect: *"Just send me your pricing and info and I'll look it over."* (analyst
frame + brush-off)

> Weak (frame lost): *"Sure! Here's a full breakdown of everything and all our
> pricing tiers…"* — now you're a brochure.
> Klaff (frame won): *"Happy to — but I only send it once I know it's actually a fit,
> otherwise it's just noise in your inbox. Quick one: what would this need to do for
> you to be a no-brainer? If it lines up, I'll show you exactly how it works."*

You stayed the prize, broke the analyst frame with a calibrated question (Voss), and
turned "send me info" into *them* qualifying the fit.

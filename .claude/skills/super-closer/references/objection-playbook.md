# Objection Playbook — worked examples

Reference examples of the full loop applied to the most common objections. Each shows
the reply plus the tactic stack behind it. These are patterns to adapt, not scripts
to paste blindly — always swap in the user's real voice and real proof.

---

## "Too expensive."

**Diagnose:** surface = price; real = value/likelihood not proven; temperature =
mild defensiveness.

**Reply (DM/email):**
> "Totally fair — you're clearly careful about where the money goes, and that's
> exactly the mindset this pays off for. Quick question though: what's it costing you
> every month those replies go out flat and deals stall? Because that's the real
> number. Last month someone in your exact spot reopened a dead $4k deal with one
> reply from this. If it doesn't [specific outcome] for you, you don't pay — I keep
> working until it does. Want me to hold your spot while you decide?"

**Why it works:**
- *Carnegie:* opened with honest appreciation, no criticism — they feel respected.
- *Voss:* calibrated "what's it costing you" surfaces the real driver.
- *Hormozi:* reframed price → cost of inaction; added a risk-reversal guarantee
  (the likelihood lever).
- *Cialdini:* social proof (someone "in your exact spot") + true scarcity ("your
  spot").
- *Greene:* the "want me to hold it while you decide" is calm, non-needy.

## "Let me think about it."

**Diagnose:** almost always an unspoken doubt or "I'm not the decision-maker."

**Reply:**
> "Of course — this should be a clear yes or it's not right. Just so I'm not leaving
> you hanging: what's the one thing still on your mind about it? Usually it's price,
> timing, or whether it'll actually work — and I'd rather answer it now than have you
> guess."

**Why it works:**
- *Voss:* calibrated question drags the real objection into the open; accusation
  audit ("price, timing, or whether it'll work") names their likely fear first.
- *Carnegie:* "this should be a clear yes" respects their judgment.
- *Greene / Klaff:* "or it's not right" keeps you the prize, not the chaser.

## "We already use [competitor]."

**Diagnose:** switching cost + loyalty + fear of hassle.

**Reply:**
> "Nice — honestly if [competitor]'s working for you, keep it, no sales pitch here.
> You're probably thinking switching anything over is a headache and not worth it.
> Fair. The only reason people move is one specific gap: [the one thing you do that
> they can't]. If that gap's costing you deals, it's worth 10 minutes. If not, stick
> with what works."

**Why it works:**
- *Voss:* accusation audit ("switching is a headache") defuses the real objection;
  mirror-friendly opener.
- *Carnegie:* zero criticism of their current choice — praised it.
- *Hormozi:* stacked the *delta* (one specific gap), not a feature war.
- *Greene:* "stick with what works" = willing to walk, kills neediness.

## Ghosting / went cold after a quote.

**Diagnose:** lost priority, or an unaddressed fear. Chasing makes it worse.

**Reply (the Voss "no"-oriented re-engagement):**
> "Have you given up on fixing the [problem] for now? Totally fine if the timing
> shifted — just let me know so I stop cluttering your inbox and free the spot."

**Why it works:**
- *Voss:* "Have you given up…?" invites a safe "No, I haven't!" that revives the
  thread.
- *Greene:* "free the spot" + willingness to disappear = strategic absence, non-needy.
- *Klaff:* you're not chasing; you're withdrawing, which pulls them back.

## "Can you do it cheaper?"

**Diagnose:** anchoring you down / testing your frame.

**Reply:**
> "I hear you. I don't really discount — it tends to mean cutting the parts that
> actually get the result, and I'd rather not set you up to be disappointed. What I
> *can* do is [remove a piece of scope so the number drops with it] or [add a
> guarantee so there's zero risk on your end]. Which of those matters more to you?"

**Why it works:**
- *Hormozi:* change the offer, never just the price — scope down or value up, never
  a naked discount.
- *Klaff:* holding the price holds the frame; you're the prize.
- *Greene:* the calm refusal to discount signals real value and non-neediness.
- *Voss:* ends on a calibrated either/or that keeps them choosing forward.

## "I need to check with my partner/boss."

**Diagnose:** real — not the final authority. Don't fight it; arm your champion.

**Reply:**
> "Makes total sense — big call to make together. Let me give you the two lines that
> usually matter to a [partner/boss]: [the outcome + the risk-reversal]. And here's
> the one result to show them: [specific proof]. Want me to put it in a short note you
> can just forward?"

**Why it works:**
- *Carnegie + Voss:* respects the process, makes it easy, no pressure.
- *Cialdini:* hands them authority + social proof to carry up the chain.
- *Commitment/consistency:* "want me to put it in a note" is a small forward yes.
